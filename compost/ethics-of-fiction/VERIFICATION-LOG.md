## ✅ 1. Booth / Moses — VERIFIED from the printed page

Source: photograph of the book supplied by PlayfulProcess, 4 Sep 2026. Moses is quoted directly.

> "It's hard for me to say this, but I have to say it anyway. I simply can't teach *Huckleberry
> Finn* again. The way Mark Twain portrays Jim is so offensive to me that I get angry in class, and

> it's right to subject students, black or white, to the many distorted views of race on which that

> about slavery and its consequences, and about how whites should deal with liberated slaves, and
> how liberated slaves should behave or will behave toward whites, good ones and bad ones. That book
> is just bad education, **and the fact that it's so cleverly written makes it even more troublesome
> to me.**"

Booth, immediately after:

> "All of his colleagues were offended: obviously Moses was violating academic norms of objectivity.
> For many of us, this was the first experience with anyone inside the academy who considered a
> literary work so dangerous that it should not be assigned to students. We had assumed that only
> 'outsiders' — those enemies of culture, the censors — talked that way about art. I can remember
> lamenting the shoddy education that had left **poor Paul Moses** unable to recognize a great
> classic when he met one. Had he not even noticed that Jim is of all the characters closest to the
> moral center? Moses obviously could neither read…"

**What this changes:** the memory was right, and better than remembered.
1. Moses **explicitly rules out the slur** as his objection — "it's the whole range of assumptions."
   That is developmental intergroup theory stated by a working teacher c. 1963.
2. **"So cleverly written makes it even more troublesome"** — the transportation argument, ~forty
   years before Green & Brock measured it. Craft as delivery mechanism, named by the person harmed.
3. "*Poor* Paul Moses" — condescension in a single adjective, preserved by the man who wrote it
   against himself. That is the essay's opening.
4. Booth's rebuttal — "Jim is of all the characters closest to the moral center" — is *exactly* the
   defence Lobato's defenders make about Nastácia. Same move, same century, already answered.

## ✅ 2. Vezzali et al. 2015 — VERIFIED, and stronger than described

*Journal of Applied Social Psychology* 45(2): 105–121.
- **Three studies**: one experimental intervention with elementary schoolchildren, plus two
  cross-sectional studies with high-school and university students. Italy and the UK.
- **Targets**: immigrants, gay people, refugees.
- **Moderation confirmed**: identification with Harry *enhances* the attitude shift; identification
  with **Voldemort hinders it**. Not just "identification matters" — the direction of identification
  flips the sign.
- **Mechanism**: perspective taking.
- Books-read count predicted perspective taking toward refugees *among participants less identified
  with Voldemort*.

**Why it now matters more:** the study did not merely show fiction can reduce prejudice. It showed
that **the same book produces opposite effects depending on which character the reader loves.** That
is the single most useful empirical fact available for the Lobato argument, because the Sítio's
love-object is Emília, and Emília is the one who says *"coisa mesmo de negra beiçuda."*
→ [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jasp.12279) ·
[full PDF, Stanford SPARQ](https://sparq.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj19021/files/media/file/vezzali_et_al._2015_-_the_greatest_magic_of_harry_potter.pdf)

## ✅ 3. La Ferrara, Chong & Duryea 2012 — VERIFIED, with a detail worth the whole essay

*American Economic Journal: Applied Economics* 4(4): 1–31.
- Identification strategy: **timing of Globo's entry into different markets.**
- Result: women in Globo-covered areas have significantly lower fertility. Strongest among **lower
  socioeconomic status** women and in the **central and late phases** of fertility — i.e. *stopping*
  behaviour, which is what you'd expect if the mechanism is aspiration rather than information.
- **Content analysis of 115 novelas (1965–1999): 72% of main female characters aged ≤50 had no
  children at all; 21% had exactly one.**
- **The mechanism evidence is the gift: children's naming patterns.** Brazilians named their babies
  after novela characters, and that is how the authors separated "novelas" from "television."

→ **Use the names.** It is the most concrete possible demonstration that Brazilians metabolise
fiction as reality — people gave their actual children the names of imaginary ones, and the birth
records prove it. You do not need to argue the novela point after that; you just cite the registry.
→ [AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.4.4.1) ·
[full PDF](https://jenni.uchicago.edu/Fertility/Ferrara_Chong_Duryea_2012_AEJAE_v4_n4.pdf)

## ⚠️ 5. Chico Xavier, *Pinga Fogo*, and Umbanda-as-white-guilt — NOT FOUND AS ASKED

**What is confirmed:** *Pinga Fogo* (TV Tupi, 28 July 1971) is real, historic, and Chico Xavier **did
discuss Umbanda, caboclos and pretos velhos** on it — there is a segment circulating under exactly
that title. A full transcript PDF exists at obrasoriginais.chicoxavier.ca (too large to fetch
automatically; download and search it by hand).

**What is *not* confirmed:** any passage where he frames Umbanda as a way for white Brazilians to
process guilt for slavery or colonialism. I could not find it, and I will not paraphrase it into
existence. Two cautions before attributing it: (a) Chico Xavier was **Kardecist**, not Umbandista,
and the Kardecist–Umbanda relationship in that period was one of careful distancing, so a generous
remark about Umbanda would already have been notable; (b) the "white guilt" framing is the shape of
*scholarship*, not of his idiom.

**Where the idea actually lives — and it is a better citation anyway:**

**Ortiz, R. *A Morte Branca do Feiticeiro Negro: Umbanda e sociedade brasileira* (1978).** `[SOLID]`
The title alone is the thesis — *the white death of the black sorcerer*. Ortiz argues Umbanda is not
a Black religion in the sense Candomblé is; it is the **fusion of two movements: the whitening of
Black culture and the blackening of Kardecist ideology** — Black, white and Indigenous elements
integrated *through white and class ideology*. Post-abolition, the enslaved population's religious
universe fractured and its elements were assimilated by the Christian tradition, producing **not the
Africanisation of Christianity in the tropics but the Christianisation of African religion**, the
only form an elite pretending to be European would accept.

→ **That is your intuition, sourced, and sharper than the version you were looking for.** The
mechanism is not guilt — it is *absorption*. And the figure where it concentrates is the **preto
velho**: an elderly enslaved spirit who counsels, forgives, and does not accuse. A white Brazilian
can kneel before a Black elder and receive absolution without ever encountering reparation.
Whether that is composting or laundering is exactly the question worth writing — and you have
standing to ask it, because you go.

Further: Diana Brown, *Umbanda: Religion and Politics in Urban Brazil* `[CHECK]`; Bastide on
Afro-Brazilian religion `[CHECK]`; and the preto-velho literature specifically `[CHECK]`.

## 📌 7. Frozen II in the frame you asked for — dying languages

Your instinct is right and it changes what the Sámi agreement means. The dub is not a courtesy; it
is **language-survival infrastructure.**

`[CHECK — get current figures from the source before printing]`
- UNESCO's endangered-languages work puts roughly **40% of the world's ~7,000 languages** at risk.
- The Sámi languages are not one language. **North Sámi** is the largest and least endangered;
  **Lule, South, Inari, Skolt** are severely endangered; **Ume, Pite and Ter Sámi** are down to very
  small speaker numbers, and at least one Sámi language has already gone silent.
- *Jikŋon 2* was dubbed into **North Sámi** — the strongest of them. Worth saying plainly rather than
  overclaiming: it served the language best placed to survive anyway.

→ **The argument this unlocks:** when a language has few enough speakers, *nothing* enters it except
by translation, and translation requires someone with money to want to. A global studio dubbing a
blockbuster into North Sámi puts contemporary, desirable, child-facing content into a language whose
children are otherwise offered almost nothing but heritage material. **That is the opposite of
extraction: it is inventory.** The appropriation critique has to answer it — and "they should have
made their own" is not an answer when the constraint is capital, not will.

**Moana:** Disney's **Oceanic Story Trust** — a standing advisory body of Pacific Islander elders,
linguists, historians, navigators and cultural practitioners. `[CHECK its composition and whether it
held actual veto or advisory power — this determines whether it is parity with the Sámi agreement or
a weaker cousin.]` Counter-example to hold honestly: the withdrawn Maui costume (2016). Also worth
checking: Moana's Pacific-language dubs (Tahitian, Māori, Hawaiian) — same inventory argument.
