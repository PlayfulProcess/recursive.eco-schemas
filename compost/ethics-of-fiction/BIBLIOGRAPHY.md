## 1. Ethical criticism — the frame

**Booth, W. C. *The Company We Keep: An Ethics of Fiction.* University of California Press, 1988.**
`[SOLID]` — the anchor. Opens with **Moses, P. B.**, a Black assistant professor at the University
of Chicago (d. 1966), who in the early 1960s refused to teach *Huckleberry Finn* and argued for its
removal from the freshman reading list. Booth and his white colleagues judged this a failure of
literary competence — a reader who couldn't recognise a classic. Decades later Booth wrote that the
whole book was "an effort to discover why that still widespread response to Paul Moses's sort of
complaint will not do," and conceded that the objection, "an overt ethical appraisal, is one
legitimate form of literary criticism" — while adding he "would of course resist anyone who tried to
ban the book from my classroom."
→ Provides: the anchor narrative; **coduction**; texts as would-be friends; the *not-banning* stance.

**VERIFIED 4 Sep 2026 — the passage, from the book itself.** Booth quotes Moses directly. The
sentence you remembered is real and it is the last one:

> "It's hard for me to say this, but I have to say it anyway. I simply can't teach *Huckleberry
> Finn* again. The way Mark Twain portrays Jim is so offensive to me that I get angry in class, and
> I can't get all those liberal white kids to understand why I am angry. What's more, I don't think
> it's right to subject students, black or white, to the many distorted views of race on which that
> book is based. No, it's not the word "n——" I'm objecting to, it's the whole range of assumptions
> about slavery and its consequences, and about how whites should deal with liberated slaves, and
> how liberated slaves should behave or will behave toward whites, good ones and bad ones. That
> book is just bad education, **and the fact that it's so cleverly written makes it even more
> troublesome to me.**"

And Booth's own reaction, immediately after:

> "All of his colleagues were offended: obviously Moses was violating academic norms of objectivity.
> For many of us, this was the first experience with anyone inside the academy who considered a
> literary work so dangerous that it should not be assigned to students. We had assumed that only
> 'outsiders' — those enemies of culture, the censors — talked that way about art. I can remember
> lamenting the shoddy education that had left poor Paul Moses unable to recognize a great classic
> when he met one."

→ **Three things this settles.** (1) Moses states the *structural* objection explicitly and rules
out the slur as his reason — "it's the whole range of assumptions." That is developmental intergroup
theory, said by a working teacher in the early 1960s. (2) **"So cleverly written makes it even more
troublesome"** is the transportation argument, forty years before Green & Brock measured it. (3)
Booth's "poor Paul Moses" is the whole essay in two words. `[SOLID — quoted from the printed page]`

`[CHECK]` Still to log: page number and edition. And do not confuse Moses with **Wallace, J. H.**,
the administrator who in the 1980s called *Huck Finn* "the most grotesque example of racist trash
ever written" — different person, different decade.

**Secondary, freely readable:**

- ["Shining a light on Paul B. Moses, trailblazing art historian," *UChicago News*](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/shining-light-paul-b-moses-trailblazing-art-historian) `[SOLID]` — he was an art historian; restores him as a person, not an anecdote.

- ["Can Huckleberry Finn Damage Readers?" *Better Living through Beowulf*](https://betterlivingthroughbeowulf.com/is-huckleberry-finn-a-racist-book/) `[CHECK]`

**Nussbaum, M. *Love's Knowledge* (1990) / *Poetic Justice* (1995).** `[SOLID]` — the parallel case

## 2. Does fiction actually move people? — narrative persuasion

**Green, M. C. & Brock, T. C. "The Role of Transportation in the Persuasiveness of Public
Narratives." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 79(5), 2000.** `[SOLID]`
→ **The hinge.** Absorption suppresses counterarguing. The better the story, the weaker the defense.
Craft is not a mitigating factor; craft is the delivery mechanism.

**Cohen, J. "Defining Identification." *Mass Communication and Society* 4(3), 2001.** `[SOLID]`
→ Persuasion runs through identification with a character. Names the route Emília travels.

**Vezzali, L., Stathi, S., Giovannini, D., Capozza, D. & Trifiletti, E. "The greatest magic of Harry
Potter: Reducing prejudice." *Journal of Applied Social Psychology* 45(2), 2015.** `[CHECK]`
→ **The best card, because it cuts both ways.** Reading Potter reduced prejudice — conditional on
identifying with Harry rather than Voldemort, and on guided discussion. Proves fiction moves real
prejudice; names the mechanism; and the mechanism runs backwards in Lobato. **Verify the
identification-dependence result itself, not just the headline.**

**La Ferrara, E., Chong, A. & Duryea, S. "Soap Operas and Fertility: Evidence from Brazil."
*American Economic Journal: Applied Economics* 4(4), 2012.** `[CHECK]`
→ Staggered Globo rollout across municipalities → causal change in fertility, tracking the family
structures depicted. **Brazilian, causal, top journal.** Adults, fully rational, all of whom knew it
was fiction. Companion: Chong & La Ferrara on divorce.

**Gerbner, G. — cultivation theory** (various, 1970s–). `[SOLID]` → background frame only.

## 4. How children acquire racial attitudes

**Bigler, R. S. & Liben, L. S. "Developmental Intergroup Theory." *Current Directions in
Psychological Science* 16(3), 2007** (and the 2006 *Advances in Child Development* chapter). `[SOLID]`
→ **The structural argument.** Children build stereotypes when an environment makes a category
perceptually salient *and* correlates it with status — with nobody saying anything prejudiced. This
is what indicts the Sítio's architecture independently of its slurs.

**Bigler, R. S. "The Use of Multicultural Curricula and Materials to Counter Racism in Children."
*Journal of Social Issues* 55(4), 1999.** `[CHECK]`

**Hughes, J. M., Bigler, R. S. & Levy, S. R. "Consequences of Learning About Historical Racism Among
European American and African American Children." *Child Development* 78(6), 2007.** `[CHECK]`
→ Children do **not** spontaneously extract the anti-racist moral. Explicit adult framing is
required; without it, effects can invert. **This is what kills "we'll just read it critically."**

**Aboud, F. *Children and Prejudice.* Blackwell, 1988.** `[SOLID]` — the developmental curve.

**Clark, K. B. & Clark, M. P. "Racial Identification and Preference in Negro Children," 1947.** `[SOLID]`
→ Where the cost lands first: self-concept, inside the category.

**Allport, G. *The Nature of Prejudice.* 1954.** `[SOLID]` — foundational; distinguishes prejudice
*adopted* from adults vs. *developed* from environmental structure. The latter is your case.

**Tatum, B. D. *Why Are All the Black Kids Sitting Together in the Cafeteria?* 1997.** `[SOLID]`
→ The **smog** metaphor — you breathe it whether or not you choose to. Best plain-language image
available for a Substack audience.

## 5. "Would a scholar of racism have said this?" — yes, and first

**Fanon, F. *Black Skin, White Masks* (1952), esp. ch. 6.** `[CHECK — verify wording and pages]`

Antillean children reading Tarzan, adventure magazines and comics *identify with the white explorer
against the "savages"* — and so acquire an anti-Black gaze which they later discover is aimed at

## 8. Beauty tracks the elite too — your intuition, sourced

**Veblen, T. *The Theory of the Leisure Class* (1899).** `[SOLID]` → the original theory, and it does
exactly the work you want: status is signalled by displaying that you **do not have to work**. A
pilates body and a winter tan are both *conspicuous leisure*. Veblen even discusses how the ideal
female body shifts to whatever signals exemption from labour.

**Strings, S. *Fearing the Black Body: The Racial Origins of Fat Phobia.* NYU Press, 2019.** `[SOLID]`
→ **The best single citation you'll get.** Argues thinness became a status marker specifically as a
device for distinguishing white bourgeois women from Black women — fatphobia as a racial project
before it was ever a health one. It joins §7 and §5 into one argument.

**Stearns, P. N. *Fat History: Bodies and Beauty in the Modern West* (1997).** `[CHECK]` → dates the
plump→thin flip in the West to roughly 1890–1920, i.e. when calories stopped being scarce.

**Gilman, S. *Fat: A Cultural History of Obesity* (2008).** `[CHECK]`

**The tan.** `[CHECK]` Pale-as-elite (indoor labour) → tanned-as-elite (leisure travel), conventionally
dated to the 1920s and popularly attributed to Chanel. **Say the sharp part:** the same shade is
prestige on a white body and penalty on a Black one, which proves the variable was never the colour.
→ **Hunter, M. *Race, Gender, and the Politics of Skin Tone* (2005)** `[SOLID]`; **Telles (2004)** for
Brazil `[SOLID]`.

## 12. Consent, consultation, and who gets to say — the Sámi case

**Disney / Sámi agreement on *Frozen II* (2019).** `[SOLID]`

## 13. Ideas multiply when shared — and the attention economy inverts it

**Jefferson, T. — letter to McPherson, I., 13 August 1813.** `[SOLID]` **Public domain.**
The canonical statement: one who receives an idea from another gains instruction without lessening
the giver's, as one who lights a taper at another's receives light without darkening him. → Use the
actual text; it's free and it's better than any paraphrase.

**Romer, P. "Endogenous Technological Change," *JPE* (1990); Nobel 2018.** `[SOLID]` → the formal
version: ideas are **non-rival**. Growth theory's whole engine.

**A Course in Miracles** — "to give is to receive"; ideas leave not their source. `[CHECK]` — locate
the exact passage; ACIM is in copyright, so paraphrase or quote briefly with attribution.

**Rosenberg, M. — Nonviolent Communication.** `[CHECK]` — that he drew on ACIM is worth one line, but
verify where he says so before asserting it.

→ **The formulation that makes this an argument rather than a sentiment:**
**ideas are non-rival; attention is rival.** The attention economy monetises the scarce half, so it
has a structural incentive to enclose the abundant half. That is why the same platform that could
give an idea to everyone at zero marginal cost instead paywalls it and sells the reader's minutes.
And it is the actual argument for CC-BY-SA and AGPL — not a preference, a diagnosis.

→ Companion: **Eghbal, N. *Working in Public* (2020)** `[SOLID]` — already cited in your own
`recursive-books-private/dashboard/`.
