## What this is
The Cary-Yale (Visconti di Modrone) deck, Milan c. 1442, Bembo workshop — the most
lavish and most unusual surviving hand-painted tarot. The card the whole project
started from (the "Damsel of Swords") lives here.

## Decisions
- **Built only from surviving cards** (those with images on Commons), because the
  deck is defined by its fragmentary, idiosyncratic survival — unlike the
  Visconti-Sforza build, where all 78 slots were created.
- **Six-court structure preserved:** King, Queen, Knight, Horsewoman (female
  knight), Knave, Damsel (female page) — half the court is women, unique to this deck.
- **Three theological virtues** (Faith, Hope, Charity) authored as their own
  category and L2 emergence — they appear in no other tarot.
- 11 trumps/virtues hand-authored (Iconography/History/Reading); pips & courts
  generated by **parsing the real Commons filenames** (`parse_card`) and
  normalising the deck's idiosyncratic suit labels (Arrows→Batons, Spades→Swords).

## Images
- Source: Wikimedia Commons, Category:Cary-Yale Visconti Tarot (73 files).
- Filenames embedded verbatim; non-card files (details, comparisons, alt scans)
  excluded by the `parse_card` filter.

## Gotchas
- Commons labels the pip suits inconsistently ("Arrows" vs "Staves", "Spades" vs
  "Swords"); normalised to four canonical suits in `SUIT_NORM`.
- Trumps are sparse (only 8 of the standard 22 survive, plus the 3 virtues).
